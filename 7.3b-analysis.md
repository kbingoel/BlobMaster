# BlobMaster training: 7.2 vs 7.3b evidence report

**Purpose:** explain why the 7.3b validation run regressed hard against the 7.2 baseline, with the numbers needed to pick the next strategy.

**TL;DR.** Four independent changes went into 7.3b (adaptive MCTS budget, value-head LR halved, looser per-epoch early stop, 21-iter horizon). All four made the run worse, and they compounded: eval win rate vs the iter-0 anchor dropped from **0.77 at iter 10 (7.2) → 0.525 at iter 10 (7.3b)**, despite 7.3b running with a richer configuration. The schedule and MCTS changes are the primary culprits; the other two compounded. Proposed next step: revert all four and re-run at 7.2's settings extended to ~15 iters, then introduce changes one at a time.

---

## 1. Run configurations

Both runs used identical self-play scale (118 games × 5P7C fixed, 44 840 examples/iter), identical bootstrap model, identical hardware (7950X + RTX 4060), and the same code for everything except the four parameters below.

| parameter | **7.2 (baseline)** | **7.3b (regressed)** |
|---|---|---|
| MCTS sims/decision | **5×100 = 500 flat** | adaptive: 3×20 (nl=2), 3×30 (nl=3), 3×100 (nl=4-6), 5×100 (nl=7+) |
| `VALUE_HEAD_LR_SCALE` | 1.0 (single group) | **0.5 (half)** |
| `epoch_early_stop_rel` | 0.005 | **0.001 (looser)** |
| `total_iterations` | 11 | **21** |
| `total_training_steps` (LR horizon) | 200 000 | 200 000 (unchanged) |

Everything else (118 games, buffer_capacity=500k, batch_size=512, epochs_per_iteration=10, peak LR=3e-4, min LR=1e-5, warmup=1000, AdamW β/wd, grad-clip=1.0) is identical.

## 4. Learning-rate trajectories

The single chart that explains most of the regression:

```
iter :  0      1      5      10     13     15     17+
7.2  : 3.0e-4 3.0e-4 2.98e-4 2.87e-4  -      -      -
7.3b : 3.0e-4 3.0e-4 2.91e-4 1.51e-4 3.9e-5 1.6e-5 ≈min_lr
```

**Mechanism.** `global_step` is incremented **twice per sampled batch** (once per head) — see [blob-nn/src/training_loop.rs:529-536](blob-nn/src/training_loop.rs#L529-L536). Cumulative steps to end of iter 10:

- **7.2 with average 3 epochs/iter** → ≈ 18 000 steps consumed (9% of 200k schedule) → LR ≈ 95% of peak.
- **7.3b with average 9.2 epochs/iter** → ≈ 102 000 steps consumed (51% of 200k) → LR ≈ 50% of peak; reaches `min_lr=1e-5` by iter 14.

The schedule horizon was never changed, but `epoch_early_stop_rel=0.001` caused 7.3b to consume ~4× more steps per iter. Iterations 14-20 in 7.3b ran at essentially zero effective LR (frozen weights).

## 5. MCTS signal ratio by branching bucket

`signal_ratio = 1 − H(visits) / ln(num_legal)`. Closer to 1 = search found a clear preference; closer to 0 = uniform visits (no signal). Averaged over all decisions in each iter (total ~44 840/iter).

### 7.2 (500 sims/decision across all buckets)

| iter | nl=2 | nl=3 | nl=4-6 | nl=7+ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|  0 | 0.54 | 0.54 | 0.47 | 0.54 |
|  1 | 0.30 | 0.32 | 0.26 | 0.33 |
|  5 | 0.30 | 0.32 | 0.30 | 0.39 |
| 10 | 0.36 | 0.38 | 0.36 | 0.40 |

### 7.3b (adaptive budget: 60 / 90 / 300 / 500 sims)

| iter | nl=2 | nl=3 | nl=4-6 | nl=7+ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|  0 | 0.46 | 0.50 | 0.71 | 0.74 |
|  1 | 0.29 | 0.31 | 0.46 | 0.55 |
|  5 | 0.26 | 0.28 | 0.40 | 0.45 |
| 10 | 0.27 | 0.29 | 0.40 | 0.41 |
| 15 | 0.26 | 0.27 | 0.38 | 0.38 |

**Interpretation.** nl=2 and nl=3 together are ≈60% of all training decisions. In 7.3b they run at 60 and 90 sims respectively — not enough to overcome the prior on a 2-way choice. The resulting visit distribution is approximately `softmax(prior × c_puct)`, i.e. the training target ≈ the policy's own output. **Zero learning signal on 60% of examples.**

7.2's uniform 500 sims/decision produced comparable signal across all buckets (0.30-0.40), with a slow upward trend from iter 1 onward — consistent with a healthy run.

## 7. Root-cause analysis

Four changes, four independent issues, all pushing in the wrong direction.

### 7.1 Adaptive MCTS starved the majority of training targets

**Evidence.** 60% of decisions (nl=2,3) were cut from 500 sims to 60-90 sims. Signal ratio on these buckets dropped ~0.1 absolute between 7.2 and 7.3b at iter 10. Bid-policy loss *rises* after iter 1 in 7.3b (overfitting on degraded targets); it *falls* monotonically in 7.2.

**Mechanism.** With c_puct=1.5 and 60 sims, the UCB selection is prior-dominated — root visits concentrate wherever the prior is already highest. Target visit distribution ≈ softmax(prior/τ). Training on that target is self-referential.

### 7.2 Halved value-head LR solved a non-problem

**Evidence.** 7.2's `grad_norms.value_head` was 4.2-5.7 throughout a run that produced **0.77 win rate at iter 10**. Calling that "too high" was mis-diagnosis. 7.3b dragged it down to 2.6-4.9 and produced 0.525 at iter 10.

**Mechanism.** The value head takes a z-scored score target with natural variance ≈ 1; its gradient magnitude is expected to exceed the policy heads'. Halving its LR decouples value from policy — MCTS backups mix Q(s,a) that uses stale value estimates while visits follow the updated policy → convergence to a fixed point where value and policy stop informing each other. The value_loss "improvement" in 7.3b (0.687 → 0.155 → 0.195) is overfitting followed by rebound, not learning.

### 7.3 Looser early-stop compounded the LR schedule

**Evidence.** 7.2 averaged 2-3 epochs/iter; 7.3b averaged 9-10. Cumulative training steps at iter 10: 7.2 ≈ 18k, 7.3b ≈ 102k. Schedule horizon (200k) unchanged. Result: 7.3b's LR hits `min_lr` at iter 14 — iters 14-20 ran at ~0 effective LR.

**Mechanism.** `total_training_steps` is a global counter calibrated against expected step consumption. Loosening early-stop without recalibrating the schedule is equivalent to making the cosine 4× steeper in time.

### 7.4 Extended horizon (21 iters) gave the schedule nowhere to go

**Evidence.** Even with 7.2's early-stop behavior, extending to 21 iters would have needed proportionally more training steps. Instead the schedule was left at 200k, so iter 15+ runs at `min_lr` regardless of early-stop setting.

## 8. Strategy: what to change for the next run

In priority order, highest-impact first. **Revert changes are safe (validated by 7.2); new changes are hypotheses.**

### 8.1 Must-revert (high confidence)

| change | from | to | reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| `adaptive_budget` | 60 / 90 / 300 / 500 | **500 flat (5×100)** | 7.2 evidence: 500 flat produces trainable signal across all buckets; 60 on nl=2 does not |
| `VALUE_HEAD_LR_SCALE` | 0.5 | **1.0** | 7.2 ran at 1.0 with grad ≈ 5 and won |
| `epoch_early_stop_rel` | 0.001 | **0.005** | 7.2 ran ~2-3 epochs/iter and won; more epochs on stale buffer = overfitting |

### 8.2 Tune for longer horizon (moderate confidence)

| change | from | to | reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| `total_iterations` | 21 | **15** | 7.2 already strong at iter 10; 15 is a comfortable margin without the schedule pressure |
| `total_training_steps` | 200 000 | **60 000** (or make it dependent on `total_iterations × avg_steps_per_iter`) | matches expected step consumption at 2-3 epochs × 15 iters |

Rationale for `total_training_steps=60 000`: 7.2 consumed ≈ 27k steps across 11 iters. Scaling linearly to 15 iters gives ≈ 37k. Rounding up to 60k leaves headroom so the schedule doesn't clip early if a few iterations run 4-5 epochs.

### 8.3 Leave unchanged

- **Games per iter = 118.** 7.2 succeeded at 118; no evidence more or fewer would help. Reducing shrinks buffer diversity; increasing costs self-play wall-clock with no known benefit.
- **Buffer capacity = 500k**, `batch_size = 512`, Adam hyperparameters, grad-clip, warmup. Unchanged since 7.2.

### 8.4 New experiments to consider (after baseline recovery)

Only after a clean 7.2-style rerun reproduces the 0.77 iter-10 result:

- **Dirichlet noise at root** (AlphaZero standard). 7.2's signal_p50_mid hovered 0.23-0.33 — exploration may still be under-provisioned. Cheap to add, standard.
- **Asymmetric MCTS budget (opposite direction)**: 5×150 instead of 5×100 on nl=7+ buckets, keeping 5×100 everywhere else. Tests whether more search on hard decisions helps, without cutting anything.
- **Fresh replay buffer strategy**: current FIFO at 500k means examples persist ~11 iters. For a 15-iter run that's fine; for longer runs it mixes stale on-policy data.

## 9. Open questions for discussion

1. **Was the original 7.2 signal_ratio plateau at ~0.35 actually healthy**, or was it already showing the fixed-point problem that 7.3b amplified? 7.2 still improved eval-wise, so *something* was working — but 10 iterations is too short to know whether it would have stalled at iter 15-20 on its own.
2. **Is `total_training_steps` the right abstraction?** It pretends batches and epochs don't exist. A cleaner design: `peak_lr` until iter N, then cosine over the last M iterations, decoupled from step count. Less fragile to epoch-count changes.
3. **Value-target z-scoring windowing.** Currently z-scored per game over all players. For games with one outlier, this compresses the signal for everyone. Worth inspecting once baseline is restored.
4. **Eval-against-anchor vs eval-against-previous-iter.** Current eval always compares to iter-0, which stops being informative once models diverge past a certain gap. A sliding-window anchor would give finer resolution on late iterations.
